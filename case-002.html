Risk Categorisation & Developer Impact

Legislation

The Bill proposes a four-tier risk framework for AI systems — prohibit, high-risk, limited-risk, and minimal-risk. High-risk systems require mandatory registration, conformity assessments, and ongoing audits. Critics argue these compliance costs disproportionately burden individual Kenyan developers and startups, while offering little corresponding consumer protection compared to existing consumer law.

Source: Government of Kenya AI Bill 2026 draft

Deepfake Penalties & Digital Rights

Digital Rights

The Bill criminalises non-consensual deepfake content with penalties up to KSh 5 million or 5 years imprisonment. Civil society groups have called for clearer definitions to prevent the same provisions from being used to chill legitimate satirical or journalistic commentary on public figures — a concern especially relevant given Kenya's recent track record of arresting bloggers and social media users for posts about government officials.

Source: Kenya ICT Action Network (KICTANet) submissions

Our Monitoring Mandate

Platform Position

Accountability to the Core will track: whether the Bill's registration requirements are applied uniformly or selectively; whether the enforcement body (proposed National AI Authority) has genuine independence; and whether public comment periods are substantive or perfunctory. We will publish updates as the Bill progresses through the National Assembly.
