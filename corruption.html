Kenya's Global Corruption Ranking

International Index

Transparency International's 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index scored Kenya at 30 out of 100, ranking it 130th among 182 countries — below the Sub-Saharan African regional average of 32. The index measures perceived levels of public-sector corruption; a score of 100 is "very clean," 0 is "highly corrupt." Kenya's score has barely moved in a decade.

Source: Transparency International via Wikipedia — Corruption in Kenya

Police Top the Bribery List — Again

EACC Survey 2025

The EACC's Kenya National Gender and Corruption Survey 2025 — conducted jointly with UNODC, KNBS, NGEC, and TI Kenya across all 47 counties — found police officers were the most frequently bribed public officials, with 35.5% of citizens who interacted with them reporting paying a bribe. Civil registration officials followed at 30%, then NTSA and immigration at 18.2% each.

Source: Nairobi Wire · Eastleigh Voice (EACC 2025)

Bribes Have More Than Doubled Since 2005

Trend Data

The national average bribe rose from KSh 3,257 in 2005 to KSh 4,878 in 2024, and to KSh 6,724 in 2025. The same survey found women face a disproportionate burden: 3.4% of women reported requests for sexual favours in exchange for services — four times the rate reported by men. The EACC termed this "corruption deepening gender inequality."

Source: EACC (2025)

KSh 65.3 Million Found in a Nairobi Official's Home

Active Case — June 2026

In June 2026, the EACC raided the home of Patrick Analo Akivaga, a suspended chief officer for urban development and planning at Nairobi City County, recovering KSh 65.3 million in cash alongside documents linked to alleged unexplained wealth. The National Integrity Alliance — a coalition including Transparency International Kenya and the Kenya Human Rights Commission — called for full prosecution and asset recovery under the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act.

Source: People Daily (Jun 2026)

The EACC's Actual Enforcement Record

Commission Performance

In FY 2024/25 alone, the EACC received 4,183 corruption complaints, opened 1,846 investigations, and forwarded just 175 case files to the Director of Public Prosecutions — a referral rate of under 10%. Since 2003, the commission has secured 293 convictions total and recovered KSh 26.65 billion in assets. Against an economy where the average official bribe occurs 12.3 million times per year based on survey data, this represents a tiny fraction of enforcement.

Source: Capital FM · Wikipedia — Corruption in Kenya (EACC)
