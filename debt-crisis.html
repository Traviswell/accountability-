Debt Crosses KSh 12.84 Trillion — Highest on Record

Treasury Data

Kenya's total public and publicly guaranteed debt hit KSh 12.84 trillion by the end of February 2026, according to the National Treasury's own monthly bulletin — pushing the debt-to-GDP ratio to 69.5%. The single-month jump of KSh 448 billion was the largest since June 2023, driven mainly by a fresh $2.25 billion Eurobond sale. Since President Ruto took office in September 2022, total debt has grown 47.6% — roughly 1.5 times faster than the economy itself.

Source: The Kenyan Wall Street (National Treasury bulletin, Feb 2026)

The IMF's Outlook Points the Wrong Way

IMF Projection

The IMF's April 2026 Regional Economic Outlook projects Kenya's debt-to-GDP ratio climbing further to 71.6% in 2026 and 72.4% in 2027, on persistent fiscal deficits estimated near 6.4% of GDP in both years. That's still slightly below the 73.4% peak hit in 2023 — but the direction is away from, not toward, sustainability.

Source: IMF April 2026 Regional Economic Outlook via The Kenyan Wall Street

A Legal Ceiling Kenya Keeps Missing

Fiscal Policy

Under the Public Finance Management (Amendment) Act 2023, Kenya committed to bringing its debt anchor down to 55% of GDP in present-value terms by 2028. Current IMF projections of 72.4% in 2027 leave roughly a 17-percentage-point gap to close in a single year — a target most independent analysts now consider effectively out of reach on the current trajectory without significant fiscal consolidation that has not yet been enacted.

Source: Institute of Economic Affairs Kenya

Where the Money Goes — and What It Costs Local Services

Budget Allocation

In FY2024/25, Kenya spent roughly KSh 1.72 trillion servicing existing debt — equivalent to approximately 69% of all ordinary revenue collected that year. This crowding-out effect directly reduces the resources available for the infrastructure investment this platform tracks failing at the local level: drainage, roads, health facilities, and water.

Source: The Kenyan Wall Street (National Treasury)

Moody's Upgrade — What It Means and What It Doesn't

Credit Markets

Moody's upgraded Kenya's sovereign credit rating to B3 (stable) on January 27, 2026 — up from Caa1 — citing reduced near-term default risk, stronger foreign-exchange reserves, and progress managing Eurobond liabilities. Analysts note the upgrade reflects a lower floor of risk rather than a fixed fiscal path. The underlying debt trajectory is still rising, and the upgrade does not alter the structural gap between revenue and expenditure.

Source: Moody's (Jan 2026) via The Kenyan Wall Street

How Kenyans Connect Debt to the Protests

Public Sentiment

A 2025 policy brief from the German Institute for International and Security Affairs (SWP Berlin) found that public discourse in Kenya frames the debt crisis less as a story of external shocks and more as one of leadership choices — pointing to wasteful spending, corruption, and impunity alongside tax hikes as the real grievances behind the 2024 Finance Bill protests and the unrest that followed into 2025.

Source: Stiftung Wissenschaft und Politik (SWP Berlin)
